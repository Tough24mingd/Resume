Cycloidal Drive

A 20:1 reduction cycloidal drive, built for higher torque than standard servo gearing.

Reduction: 20:1 Status: ~90% complete Rollers: 686-2RS bearings CAD: SolidWorks

Why a cycloidal drive

After finishing work on the robotic arm, I wanted to build something that could handle higher torque than standard servo gearing allows. A cycloidal drive trades speed for a large step up in torque, with very little backlash — a good next step from what I'd learned building the arm.

Design

The drive uses the howtomechatronics.com DIY cycloidal drive design as a starting reference — a PLA disc riding on 686-2RS ball bearing rollers — scaled up from that tutorial's 16 pins to reach a 20:1 reduction ratio. Every part is modeled in SolidWorks.

  • Ring gear rollers are 686-2RS ball bearings, 13mm OD / 6mm ID.
  • Output holes on the disc are sized at 16mm — a 13mm output bearing plus a 2×1.5mm eccentricity offset.

Status

Currently about 90% complete. The design work remaining is adding the second disc — cycloidal drives typically run two discs, offset in phase, to balance the load.
